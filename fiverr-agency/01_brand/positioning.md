# Positioning: Red Pen & Pixel

Built with Mark Pollard's Four Points (Problem, Insight, Advantage, Strategy) and his BUMP/TUBER angle test. The frameworks are his. The words below are this studio's own.

**Key word that runs through all four points: slip.**

## Four Points

| Point | Line |
|---|---|
| Problem | A small shop pays three strangers for three jobs, and the slips land in the gaps between them. |
| Insight | Loud mistakes are cheap. Quiet ones cost. (An ugly banner gets fixed by lunch. A 30 that should be 60 sits in a signed contract until the money doesn't arrive.) |
| Advantage | Every file comes back with a caught-it sheet: each slip, where it was, what I did about it. |
| Strategy | Show that a caught-it sheet is a receipt for the slips you never paid for. |

## Named things (reuse them everywhere)

- **Quiet mistake / loud mistake.** A loud mistake looks wrong, so somebody fixes it. A quiet mistake looks fine, so it ships. I sell the hunt for quiet ones.
- **Caught-it sheet.** The list that comes back with every order.

## Angle test (TUBER)

| Test | Pass? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| True | Yes | The Legal Catch-Net test caught 14 of 14 planted slips. The email master test found a real charset bug. |
| Unexpected | Yes | Most sellers sell the making. This sells the checking. |
| Broad | Yes | Every shop owner has shipped a quiet mistake. |
| Easy | Yes | "I catch what slips." Five words. |
| Relevant | Yes | Every gig is a different place a slip hides. |

**Core angle:** I catch what slips.
**Words to own:** slip, catch, read twice, caught-it sheet, quiet mistake.

## Limits to admit (in the copy, not hidden)

- One person, so fewer orders.
- The 14-of-14 result is my own test on my own dummy contract.
- AI does first passes on product sheets, prompt packs and contract comparison. I check every line.
