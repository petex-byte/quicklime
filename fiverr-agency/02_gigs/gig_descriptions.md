# Gig descriptions

Paste-ready. Written with the Mark Pollard style method (his Four Points framework and "pineapple words" are his; the key word, the caught-it sheet and the quiet-mistake idea are this studio's own). Positioning in `01_brand/positioning.md`.

Fiverr limits checked 21 Oct 2026 (help.fiverr.com, "Creating a Gig"): description up to 1,200 characters, up to 5 tags, up to 10 FAQs, up to 3 images (min 712 x 430 px), video 20 to 60 seconds under 50 MB, up to 2 PDFs. Titles are in `gig_titles_and_tags.csv`.

## Gig 1 · App and web screens (Figma) (914 characters)

```text
A developer spends three weeks building your app. Then you tap through it on your own phone and the Pay button is hiding under the order summary. That's three more weeks.

Click it before you build it.

What I do:
1. Design your screens in Figma at phone, small laptop and desktop widths.
2. Link them into a click-through prototype you can test on your own phone.
3. Build real components with auto layout, so your developer reuses them instead of guessing.
4. Write notes for your developer: spacing, colours, fonts, every state of every button.

Every order comes back with a caught-it sheet: each problem I found on the way, where it was, and what I changed.

I'm one person, so I take a few projects at a time and look at every screen twice.

Not included: coding your app. I hand over the Figma file and the notes.

Send me a sketch, a competitor's app or a voice note. Any of those is a fine place to start.
```

## Gig 2 · Sale emails (Figma + HTML + Klaviyo) (895 characters)

```text
Black Friday, 6 am. Your sale email goes to your whole list. In Outlook on Windows the button turns into plain text. In dark mode your logo disappears. Nobody tells you. They just don't buy.

Every button works. Every inbox.

What I do:
1. Design the email in Figma, matched to your brand.
2. Code it by hand in HTML, with buttons Outlook can draw.
3. Test it in Gmail, Outlook and Apple Mail, on a phone and a laptop, light and dark.
4. Set up the Klaviyo parts: first-name fallback, a unique coupon per person, a cart block that hides when the cart is empty.

You get the Figma file, the HTML file and a caught-it sheet listing every problem I found and fixed.

I only touch your Klaviyo account with your go-ahead, and I tell you exactly what I changed.

Not included: writing your sale copy from scratch. Send me your words.

Send me the last email that flopped. It's usually the best brief.
```

## Gig 3 · Contract proofreading (English and Afrikaans) (919 characters)

```text
Your supplier agreement says 30 days in English. The Afrikaans copy says 60 dae. Both are signed. Which one gets you paid?

Quiet mistakes don't look wrong. That's why they get signed.

What I do:
1. Read your contract line by line, in English, Afrikaans or both side by side.
2. Check every date, amount, name, clause number and cross-reference.
3. Hold each clause against its other-language version and flag every place they differ.
4. Fix typos and layout. Where the meaning differs, I ask which you meant. I don't pick for you.

You get a tracked-changes copy and a caught-it sheet: each finding, where it is, and how serious it is.

I use AI for a first comparison pass. I check every finding by hand before you see it.

This is proofreading and consistency checking, not legal advice. I don't tell you what to sign. For that, speak to an attorney.

Send the file. Scanned PDFs are fine if every page is readable.
```

## Gig 4 · Product data cleanup (774 characters)

```text
Your supplier sends 400 products in a spreadsheet. Some prices start with an R, some with a space, three have no price at all. Your launch is on Friday.

Messy sheet in. Live store out.

What I do:
1. Turn your sheet into a Shopify or WooCommerce import file (CSV, plus JSON if you need it).
2. Fix prices, sizes, SKUs, variants and image links.
3. Flag every row I can't fix without guessing. I never guess.
4. Count every change, so you can check my work in five minutes.

You get the import file, a change log and a caught-it sheet listing every problem row.

AI does the first pass. I check the row counts and spot-check rows by hand before delivery.

Not included: uploading to your store or writing product descriptions.

Send the sheet as it is. Don't clean it first.
```

## Gig 5 · Brand voice prompt library (898 characters)

```text
You ask AI for a product description. It sends back "Elevate your everyday with our premium collection." Your customers would never say that. Neither would you.

Your voice, on tap.

What I do:
1. Read your website, emails and posts, and pull out how you actually sound.
2. Write a voice guide: words you use, words you never use, and before-and-after examples from your own copy.
3. Build a library of AI prompts for your shop: product descriptions, emails, replies to customers.
4. Set it up as a Claude Project, so everyone who writes for you gets the same voice.

You get the voice guide, the prompt library and a caught-it sheet with every gap I found in your current copy.

AI writes the first drafts. I read and test every prompt before delivery. Afrikaans copy welcome.

Not included: running your social accounts.

Send me five things you've written that you're proud of, and one you hate.
```
