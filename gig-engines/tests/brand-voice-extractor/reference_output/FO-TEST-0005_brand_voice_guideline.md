# Brand Voice Guideline: Karoo Home

Draft for human check. Quotes carry their source file.

## 1. Core Identity

**Voice statement:** We sound like a friend in Prince Albert who has opinions about linen, talking to a regular at the Saturday market about things made to last.

**Who we talk to:** Mostly women aged 30 to 55 who shop at markets, care where things come from and pay more for things that last. In her words: "mostly women 30-55, shop at markets, care where things come from, will pay more for stuff that lasts" [notes.txt]. (Her notes, not data.)

**Three voice traits**

1. **We are warm and personal, not corporate.** A real person is behind every line, and we name them.
   - Proof: "phone us. A real person answers. Usually Annelie." [about.txt]; "Thabo cutting 40 napkins before lunch. Show-off." [instagram.txt]
   - Do: "We buy our linen from one mill and we know the people who run it." [about.txt]
   - Don't: *Our team will be in touch regarding your enquiry.*
2. **We are plain-spoken, not posh.** Everyday words, short sentences, nothing fancy.
   - Proof: "Wash cold, hang dry, skip the iron." [product-linen-shirt.txt]; "That's the whole trick." [home.txt]
   - Do: "Made slowly, in small runs, in Prince Albert." [home.txt]
   - Don't: *Our products are made with great care for people who expect the best.*
3. **We are cheeky and sure of ourselves, with a dry end-line, not loud or gag-heavy.** Say it straight, add a short tease, stop. This is where her "funny" lives for now.
   - Proof: "We won't tell." [product-linen-shirt.txt]; "It doesn't care." [product-tea-towel.txt]
   - Do: "It wrinkles. Linen likes it that way." [product-linen-shirt.txt]
   - Don't: *Say hello to the softest linen ever!!*

**What we never sound like**
- A glossy shop ad. She says "never posh." [notes.txt].
- A company letter: "our team", "regarding your enquiry".
- Loud: no exclamation marks, no shouting about a sale.

**Language rules**
- Spelling: en-ZA (colour, organise). Reader: "you".
- Contractions always (didn't, won't).
- Afrikaans: English first, at most one word per message, at the start ("Sondag in...").
- Product names: Title Case on product pages ("Linen Shirt - Olive"), lower case in running text. Capitalise Karoo Home, Prince Albert, Annelie, Thabo.
- Numbers: R750, R1,299 (R, no space, comma). Small counts in words ("six of us"), larger as digits ("40 napkins").
- Punctuation: full stops, colon as a lead-in. No exclamation marks.
- Emojis: none, anywhere, for now. She says "NEVER use emojis, they look cheap" [notes.txt].

## 3. Tone Scale

| Scale | Score | Reason and quote |
|---|---|---|
| Funny ●●●○○ Serious (= 3) | 3 | "a bit cheeky" is the brief. Dry jokes in the last words: "Show-off." [instagram.txt] |
| Casual ●●○○○ Formal (= 2) | 2 | Spoken and short: "Come say hi." [instagram.txt] |
| Irreverent ●●○○○ Respectful (= 2) | 2 | Teases shops and itself: "We don't do seasons." [about.txt] |
| Enthusiastic ●●●●○ Matter-of-fact (= 4) | 4 | Warm, never shouting: "Your size won't last long." [instagram.txt] |

| Channel | Shift | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Product page | Cheekiest. Admit the flaw, flip it | "Linen likes it that way." [product-linen-shirt.txt] |
| Sale email | More matter-of-fact. News first | *The olive shirt is back. Free delivery over R750.* |
| Instagram caption | Most casual. Scene first | "Kettle on, linen on the line." [instagram.txt] |
| Customer service, happy | Warmest. Short | *Glad it's holding up. Come say hi at the market.* |
| Customer service, complaint | Most serious. No jokes | *Sorry, that's not right. Phone us. A real person answers.* |
| Delay / out of stock | Honest, matter-of-fact | *It's sold out for now. We make things until they run out, then we make them again.* |

## 4. Before and After

**Example 1. Website home page**

**Before** (her own site, written by an agency):
> "Elevate your living space with our curated collection of premium homeware. Our premium products are crafted with the highest quality materials for the discerning customer." [home.txt]

**After:** Linen, tea towels and throws, made slowly, in small runs, in Prince Albert. We cut every piece by hand. We don't do seasons.

- Words to Ban: every posh word is gone; real products are named.
- Trait 1: "We" speaks and the hand-cutting is specific.
- Trait 2: short, plain sentences.
- Trait 3: ends on a flat, dry opinion.

**Example 2. Linen product page**

**Before** (copy she hates, from a competitor; her own material had no more weak lines):
> "Indulge in the ultimate luxury experience. Our exquisite, artisanal linens are meticulously curated to elevate your sanctuary and unlock a world of effortless elegance." [hated-competitor.txt]

**After:** Linen that gets softer every wash. We cut every piece by hand, in small runs, in Prince Albert. Skip the iron.

- Words to Ban: every competitor cliché is gone.
- Trait 1: names who did the work, and where.
- Trait 3: closes on a three-word care tip.
- Tone Scale: Matter-of-fact 4, with no big claims.

**Example 3. Instagram caption**

**Before** (Generic AI-style draft (written for this example); her own captions had no weak line left):
> Introducing our stunning new throws! ✨ Crafted for the discerning home, they'll elevate your space. Shop now!

**After:** New throws just landed. Heavy, soft, and the colour of the veld after rain. Made slowly, in small runs, in Prince Albert.

- Words to Ban: hype and posh words gone.
- Language rules: no exclamation marks, no emoji.
- Trait 1: names the place; no "Shop now".
- Tone Scale: Casual 2, Enthusiastic 4. A plain statement with one local image.

## 5. Quick Rules

1. Write like Annelie over the kettle: short words, about six per sentence.
2. Name the person, the place or the mill. Never "our team".
3. Say it straight, add one dry line, stop.
4. Admit the flaw, then flip it.
5. No exclamation marks.
6. No emojis. Anywhere.
7. A word on the Words to Ban list means rewrite the sentence.
8. One Afrikaans word at most, at the start.
9. en-ZA spelling, prices as R1,299, call the reader "you".
10. Only say what is true. No fact, no claim.
