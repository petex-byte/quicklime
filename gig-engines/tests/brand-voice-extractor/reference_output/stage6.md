# Brand Voice Guideline: Karoo Home

Draft for human check before it goes to the client. Built from the client's own copy (website, product pages, Instagram) and her notes. Quotes are tagged with the file they came from.

## 1. Core Identity

**Voice statement**

We sound like a friend in Prince Albert who has opinions about linen, talking to a regular at the Saturday market about things made to last.

**Who we talk to**

Mostly women aged 30 to 55 who shop at markets, care where things come from and will pay more for things that last. In the client's words: "mostly women 30-55, shop at markets, care where things come from, will pay more for stuff that lasts" [notes.txt]. The copy backs this up in small ways: "Bring your own bag." [instagram.txt] and "She wanted tea towels that didn't fall apart after three washes." [about.txt]. Note that this picture comes from her notes, not from data. See section 6.

**Three voice traits**

1. **We are warm and personal, not corporate.**
   - Means: a real person is behind every line, and we name them.
   - Proof: "phone us. A real person answers. Usually Annelie." [about.txt]
   - Proof: "Behind the scenes: Thabo cutting 40 napkins before lunch. Show-off." [instagram.txt]
   - Also: "We buy our linen from one mill and we know the people who run it." [about.txt]
   - Do: "Thabo cut 40 napkins before lunch."
   - Don't: *Our team will be in touch regarding your enquiry.*

2. **We are plain-spoken, not posh.**
   - Means: everyday words, short sentences, nothing fancy.
   - Proof: "Wash cold, hang dry, skip the iron." [product-linen-shirt.txt]
   - Proof: "Linen that gets softer every time you wash it. That's the whole trick." [home.txt]
   - Also: "Returns within 30 days, no fuss." [home.txt]
   - Do: "Made slowly, in small runs, in Prince Albert." [home.txt]
   - Don't: *Our products are made with great care for people who expect the best.*

3. **We are cheeky and sure of ourselves, with a dry end-line, not loud or gag-heavy.**
   - Means: say it straight, add a short tease, stop. No puns, no exclamation marks. This is where the client's "funny" lives for now (see section 6).
   - Proof: "Wear it to the market, wear it to a wedding, wear it to bed. We won't tell." [product-linen-shirt.txt]
   - Proof: "Wash it hot, wash it cold. It doesn't care." [product-tea-towel.txt]
   - Also: "We don't do seasons." [about.txt]
   - Do: "It wrinkles. Linen likes it that way." [product-linen-shirt.txt]
   - Don't: *Say hello to the softest linen ever!!*

**What we never sound like**

- Never like a glossy shop ad. The client says "never posh." [notes.txt], and if a line could sit in a catalogue, rewrite it.
- Never like a company letter: no "our team", no "regarding your enquiry", no stock phrases.
- Never loud: no exclamation marks, no pictures in the middle of a sentence, no shouting about a sale.

**Language rules**

- **Spelling:** en-ZA (colour, organise, favourite). Evidence: "the colour of the veld after rain" [instagram.txt].
- **Addressing the reader:** "you". Never "u". Use "jy" only if the client asks for a full Afrikaans line (none in the material).
- **Contractions:** always (didn't, don't, won't, doesn't, that's). No "do not" or "does not".
- **Afrikaans:** English first. At most one Afrikaans word per caption or message, at the start ("Sondag in...", "Lekker news:"). The material has two Afrikaans words in total.
- **Product names:** on a product page, Title Case with a hyphen ("Linen Shirt - Olive", "Waffle Tea Towel - Set of 2"). In running text, lower case ("the olive shirt", "tea towel", "throws", "napkins"). Always capitalise Karoo Home, Prince Albert, Annelie and Thabo.
- **Prices and numbers:** "R" then the number, no space, comma for thousands: R750, R1,299, R249. Small counts in words ("six of us", "three washes", "one mill"). Larger counts and days as digits ("40 napkins", "30 days", "100% linen"). Years as digits.
- **Punctuation:** full stops do most of the work. A colon is fine as a lead-in ("Lekker news:", "Behind the scenes:"). No exclamation marks. No dashes.
- **Sentence length:** aim for about six words on average. Fragments are fine ("Usually Annelie.").
- **Emojis:** none, anywhere, for now. The client says "NEVER use emojis, they look cheap" [notes.txt]. Her Instagram does use them, so see section 6.

## 4. Before and After

**Example 1. Website home page (hero)**

**Before** (the client's own site, written by an agency):
> "Elevate your living space with our curated collection of premium homeware. Our premium products are crafted with the highest quality materials for the discerning customer." [home.txt]

**After:**
> Linen, tea towels and throws, made slowly, in small runs, in Prince Albert. We cut every piece by hand. We don't do seasons.

What changed:
- Trait 2 (plain-spoken, not posh): the posh words are gone, and the real products are named instead.
- Trait 1 (warm and personal): "We" speaks, and the hand-cutting is specific. The reader is spoken to like a neighbour, not a target.
- Trait 3 (cheeky, dry end-line): ends on a short, flat opinion.
- Facts used: "Made slowly, in small runs, in Prince Albert." [home.txt]; "We still cut every piece by hand." and "We don't do seasons." [about.txt]; tea towels [product-tea-towel.txt]; throws [instagram.txt].

**Example 2. Instagram caption**

**Before** (the client's own caption, which breaks her rule on emojis):
> "Market this Saturday at the Prince Albert Village Market. Come say hi. Bring your own bag. 🛍️" [instagram.txt]

**After:**
> Market this Saturday at the Prince Albert Village Market. Come say hi. Bring your own bag.

What changed:
- Language rules: the emoji is removed. Emojis are banned everywhere for now.
- Tone Scale: stays Casual 2 and Matter-of-fact 4. Three plain sentences, no exclamation mark.
- Trait 1 (warm and personal): "Come say hi." and the friendly bossiness of "Bring your own bag." stay.
- This one was already close to the voice. The emoji is the only real fix.

**Example 3. Linen product page**

**Before** (copy the client hates, from a competitor, used because her own material had no weak product line left):

- Trait 1 (warm and personal): "We cut every piece by hand" shows who did the work. The old line was about the reader's home, not about people.
- Trait 3 (cheeky, dry end-line): ends on a three-word care tip.
- Facts used: "Linen that gets softer every time you wash it." [home.txt]; "We still cut every piece by hand." [about.txt]; "Made slowly, in small runs, in Prince Albert." ## 5. Quick Rules

1. Write like Annelie talking over the kettle: short words, short sentences, about six words each.
2. Name the person, the place or the mill. Never "our team".
3. Say it straight, add one dry line, stop.
4. Admit the flaw, then flip it ("It wrinkles. Linen likes it that way.").
5. No exclamation marks.
6. No emojis. Anywhere.
7. If a word is on the Words to Ban list, rewrite the sentence. If it sounds like a shop ad, rewrite it.
8. One Afrikaans word at most, at the start. English first.
9. Spell it en-ZA (colour), write prices as R1,299, and call the reader "you".
10. Only say what is true. If you don't have the fact, ask. Never guess a date, an award or a number.
