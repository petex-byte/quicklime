### Example 1. Website home page (hero)

**Before** (type a: client's own site, written by an agency, which she says "doesn't sound like us at all") [home.txt]:
> "Elevate your living space with our curated collection of premium homeware. Our premium products are crafted with the highest quality materials for the discerning customer."

**After:**
> Linen, tea towels and throws, made slowly, in small runs, in Prince Albert. We cut every piece by hand. We don't do seasons.

What changed:
- Trait 2 (plain-spoken, not posh): removed "elevate", "curated", "premium", "crafted", "highest quality", "discerning" and "homeware", all on the Words to Ban list. Named the real products instead.
- Trait 1 (warm and personal): "We" speaks, and the hand-cutting is real and specific. The reader is no longer "the discerning customer".
- Trait 3 (cheeky, dry end-line): finished on a short flat line with an opinion, "We don't do seasons."
- Facts used: "Made slowly, in small runs, in Prince Albert." [home.txt]; "We still cut every piece by hand." and "We don't do seasons." [about.txt]; tea towels [product-tea-towel.txt]; throws [instagram.txt]; Linen [home.txt].

### Example 2. Instagram caption

**Before** (type a: client's own caption, which breaks her "NEVER use emojis" rule) [instagram.txt, Caption 5]:
> Market this Saturday at the Prince Albert Village Market. Come say hi. Bring your own bag. [shopping bags emoji]

**After:**
> Market this Saturday at the Prince Albert Village Market. Come say hi. Bring your own bag.
