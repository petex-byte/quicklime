# Brand Voice Guideline: Karoo Home

## 1. Core Identity

**Voice statement:** We sound like the owner of a small Prince Albert homeware business, talking to a regular at the Saturday market about the linen she made this week.

**Who we talk to:** Mostly women aged 30 to 55, per Annelie: "customers: mostly women 30-55, shop at markets, care where things come from, will pay more for stuff that lasts" [notes.txt]. [CONFIRM: check against real customer data.]

**Trait 1. We are plain-spoken, not posh.** Short words, real things, no fancy nouns.
- "We don't do seasons." [about.txt]
- "That's the whole trick." [home.txt]
- Do: "We cut every piece by hand." Don't: "Each piece is shaped by our skilled team."

**Trait 2. We are warm like a neighbour, not like a brand.** We name people and give a clear next step.
- "A real person answers. Usually Annelie." [about.txt]
- "Come say hi. Bring your own bag." [instagram.txt]
- Do: "Phone us. A real person answers." Don't: "Please contact our support team."

**Trait 3. We are cheeky, not clownish.** One dry wink, at the end.
- "Wear it to the market, wear it to a wedding, wear it to bed. We won't tell." [product-linen-shirt.txt]
- "Behind the scenes: Thabo cutting 40 napkins before lunch. Show-off." [instagram.txt]
- Do: "It wrinkles. Linen likes it that way." Don't: "Wrinkles?! LOL, we love them!!"

**What we never sound like:**
1. A catalogue: long sentences, big nouns, no people.
2. A call centre: stiff apologies, no name, no phone number.
3. A stand-up comic: puns, shouting, exclamation marks.

**Language rules**

- **Spelling:** en-ZA: "colour" [instagram.txt]; organise, not organize.
- **Address:** "you" and "your" for the reader; "we" for the shop. Never "the customer".
- **Afrikaans:** English first. One common word per post at most.
- **Product names:** capitalised as page titles ("Waffle Tea Towel - Set of 2"), lower case in sentences ("the olive shirt" [instagram.txt]).
- **Numbers and prices:** R then digits, comma for thousands, no space: R750, R1,299, R249. Digits for 30 days and 40 napkins; words for small counts ("six of us").
- **Punctuation:** contractions yes; no exclamation marks.
- **Emoji:** none, anywhere, for now. [CONFIRM: see section 6.]

## 3. Tone Scale

- **Funny** ●●○○○ **Serious** (= 2). A dry wink. The client wants 2; the material shows about 3. "We won't tell." [product-linen-shirt.txt]
- **Casual** ●●○○○ **Formal** (= 2). Contractions and fragments. "Wash it hot, wash it cold. It doesn't care." [product-tea-towel.txt]
- **Irreverent** ●●●○○ **Respectful** (= 3). Teases product and team, never the customer. "Show-off." [instagram.txt]
- **Enthusiastic** ●●●●○ **Matter-of-fact** (= 4). Good news is plain news. "New throws just landed." [instagram.txt]

| Channel | Shift | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Product page | Plainest. Facts first. | "Dries a whole rack of dishes before it gives up." [product-tea-towel.txt] |
| Sale email | Chattier, speaks to "you". | "Your size won't last long." [instagram.txt] [CONFIRM: sale offer and dates] |
| Instagram caption | Loosest. Fragments, a scene. | "Kettle on, linen on the line." [instagram.txt] |
| Customer service (happy) | Warmest. Invites them in. | "So glad you like it. Come say hi." (proposed) |
| Customer service (complaint) | No wink. Owns it. | "That's not right, and we're sorry. Phone us." (proposed) |
| Delivery delay / out of stock | Honest, short. | "Sold out. We make things until they run out." (proposed) |

## 4. Before and After

**Example 1**
**Channel:** website home page
**Before** (agency copy) [home.txt]: "Elevate your living space with our curated collection of premium homeware." "Our premium products are crafted with the highest quality materials for the discerning customer."
**After:** "Made slowly, in small runs, in Prince Albert. We buy our linen from one mill and we know the people who run it. Linen that gets softer every time you wash it. That's the whole trick."
**What changed:**
- Every word from the Words to Ban table (section 2) is gone.
- Trait 1: vague nouns became real things.
- Tone (Casual 2): long sentences became short ones.

**Example 2**
**Channel:** product page (linen shirt)
**Before** (competitor copy the client hates) [hated-competitor.txt]: "Indulge in the ultimate luxury experience. Our exquisite, artisanal linens are meticulously curated to elevate your sanctuary and unlock a world of effortless elegance."
**After:** "100% linen, cut loose, so it moves when you do. It wrinkles. Linen likes it that way. Wash cold, hang dry, skip the iron."
**What changed:**
- Every word from the Words to Ban table (section 2) is gone.
- Trait 1: claims became facts.
- Trait 3: the flaw is owned with one dry line.

**Example 3**
**Channel:** customer service (complaint)
**Before** (Generic AI-style draft, written for this example): "Dear Valued Customer, We sincerely apologize for any inconvenience you may have experienced with your recent order. Please rest assured that our dedicated team is working diligently to resolve this matter at the earliest opportunity. We appreciate your patience and understanding."
**After:** "That's not right, and we're sorry. Phone us: a real person answers, usually Annelie. [CONFIRM: phone number]"
**What changed:**
- The cold greeting is gone (Words to Ban); "apologize" became "sorry" (en-ZA, Casual 2).
- Trait 2: a faceless team became a named person: "A real person answers. Usually Annelie." [about.txt].
- Tone: formal sentences became two, with a next step and no wink.

## 5. Quick Rules

1. Write it the way you'd say it across a market stall.
2. Keep sentences short: about 7 words.
3. Name real things: linen, the mill, Prince Albert, Annelie.
4. Say "you" and "your". Never "the customer".
5. Own the flaw, then one dry wink at the end. One per piece.
6. No exclamation marks. No emoji, for now.
7. One Afrikaans word per post at most.
8. Spell it en-ZA (colour, organise). Write prices as R1,299.
9. Complaints get no wink: say sorry, say what happens next.
10. Check the Words to Ban table. If a competitor could say it, don't.
