30 days or 60 dae?

Loud mistakes are cheap. Quiet ones cost.

A loud mistake looks wrong, so somebody fixes it by lunch. A quiet mistake looks fine. It ships, gets signed, goes out to nine thousand people. You find it when the money doesn't arrive.

  1. The Black Friday email goes out with a dead button.
  2. The supplier contract says 30 days in English and 60 dae in Afrikaans.
  3. Four hundred products go live with last month's price.
  4. The checkout looks fine on a laptop and hides the Pay button on an iPhone SE.
  5. None of these look wrong. That's why they ship.

Outlook redecorates sale emails.

Unless someone stops it. This is my Klaviyo master template, running live. Scroll inside it. I design it in Figma, then code it by hand, one block at a time.

  1. Outlook on Windows. Every button is drawn twice, once in HTML and once in Outlook's own shapes, so it never turns into a bare link.
  2. Dark mode. The logo swaps to a light version and the coupon stays readable.
  3. Phones. Two columns become one under 600 pixels. Every button is at least 44 pixels tall, big enough for a thumb.
  4. Names. "Anika, it's here." When Klaviyo has no first name, it says "Hello" instead of a blank.
  5. Coupons. Each person gets their own code, so one leaked code can't empty the sale.
  6. Empty carts. The "Still in your bag" block hides itself when there's no cart.
